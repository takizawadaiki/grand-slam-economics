11 — Research Methodology

Methodology &
Assumptions

TennisIQ adheres to a transparent research standard. All estimates are labelled and explained. Primary sources are used wherever available.

Data Classification Standards
OfficialPrize money from ATP/WTA/tournament announcements. AELTC (Companies House), Tennis Australia (ASIC), USTA (Form 990).
EstimatedTournament revenues where full accounts unavailable. Derived from Sportico, Forbes Sports, SportsPro, and media reporting. Confidence varies.
ModelledPlayer revenue shares, CIS™ scores, Lorenz curves, Survival Simulator outputs, savings trajectories. Built from proxy data with documented assumptions.
CPI Adj.US BLS CPI-U All Items Index, base 2000. Non-USD converted via Fed Reserve H.10 annual average FX rates before deflation.
Confidence & Limitations

Grand Slam tournaments do not publish audited consolidated revenues. Revenue estimates rely on partial disclosures and third-party analytics. Confidence is highest for Wimbledon and Australian Open where annual accounts are filed; lowest for Roland Garros where FFT disclosure is partial.

Player expense data is self-reported. Costs vary substantially by nationality, ranking, and management structure. The Financial Survival Simulator uses median-case assumptions.

CIS™ and sustainability scores are modelled composites. They do not constitute official valuations. Updated quarterly.

Revenue Estimation Methodology+

Tournament revenue estimates use multi-source triangulation. Official accounts (AELTC, Tennis Australia) are used directly. USTA Form 990 nonprofit filings provide partial data, supplemented by Sportico and Forbes valuations. Confidence intervals are widest for Roland Garros, where the FFT does not publish itemised revenue.

Est. Revenue = Broadcasting Rights + Sponsorship Portfolio + Ticket & Hospitality + Merchandise & Other

Broadcasting values estimated from publicly announced deal structures (Amazon, ESPN+, Eurosport, beIN) divided across tournaments based on market allocations.

Inflation Adjustment (CPI) Methodology+

All real value calculations use US BLS CPI-U All Items Index with base year 2000. Non-USD prize money (GBP, EUR, AUD) is converted to USD using Fed H.10 annual average FX rates before deflation — a deliberate choice that measures purchasing power against a consistent US dollar baseline.

Real Value (2000 USD) = Nominal Value × (CPI_2000 / CPI_year)

Cumulative CPI inflation 2000→2025: approximately +83% (BLS CPI-U). This means $1M in 2025 prize money is worth approximately $547K in 2000 purchasing power.

Commercial Impact Score™ Methodology+

CIS™ is a proprietary composite from ATP/WTA ranking data, publicly disclosed sponsorship announcements, social media analytics (Instagram, TikTok, X/Twitter), broadcast mention tracking, and search volume indices (Google Trends).

CIS = 0.25(P) + 0.20(S) + 0.20(E) + 0.15(V) + 0.12(A) + 0.08(T)

P=Performance, S=Sponsorship, E=Engagement, V=Visibility, A=Attendance, T=Trend Velocity. Scores are updated quarterly. All values indicative only.

Financial Survival Simulator Assumptions+

Prize income is modelled from ATP ranking-tier distributions. Tier bases: Top 10 ($280K/tournament), 11–25 ($95K), 26–50 ($42K), 51–100 ($18K), 101–150 ($8K), 151–250 ($3.5K), 251–400 ($1.8K), 400+ ($0.9K). Win rate multiplier: 0.5 + (winRate/100). Debt trajectory assumes 8% annual interest on any deficit. Burnout risk: weighted function of weeks-away, net income deficit, and career year. All outputs indicative.

Executive Summary
TennisIQ Brief · Q2 2026 · AI-Generated

Grand Slam Revenue Dynamics: Combined revenues across all four Grand Slams have grown an estimated +412% since 2000, reaching approximately $1.89B USD in 2024. This growth is overwhelmingly driven by broadcasting and streaming rights, now representing ~52% of total revenue. The next rights cycle is projected to deliver a further 30–50% uplift.

Player Compensation Structure: Nominal prize money grew +287% over the same period, while real (CPI-adjusted) prize money grew just +94%. Revenues have grown at 4.4× the rate of real player compensation. The implied player revenue share of approximately ~18% compares unfavourably to the 48–50% guaranteed in major North American team sports via CBAs.

Career Economic Viability: For the majority of professional players, a touring career is financially unsustainable without supplemental sponsorship. Players ranked outside the top 100 face estimated prize income of $80–140K against $115–200K in typical costs. TennisIQ models estimate approximately 60% of touring professionals operate at a net annual deficit. Challenger-level net income: estimated -$55K per year. High burnout risk is documented above rank 150.

Commercial Concentration: Commercial value is highly concentrated. TennisIQ’s CIS™ leaderboard shows Alcaraz (94.2) and Sinner (91.8) leading commercial value, while the wider field receives negligible sponsorship. Gini coefficient of ~0.81 for prize distribution vs ~0.48 for NBA salary distribution.

Strategic Outlook: The structural gap between revenue growth and player compensation is likely to widen absent a formal collective bargaining mechanism. At NBA-style revenue sharing (50%), the combined 4-Slam prize pool would approximate $944M — nearly 3× current levels. The PTPA (est. 2020) remains without formal governing body recognition.
