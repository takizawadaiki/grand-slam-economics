# Data sources

Retrieved 16 November 2026.

| File | Source | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `wimbledon_prize_money.csv` | Wikipedia, "Wimbledon Championships", prize money table, which cites the All England Club's yearly announcements. 2018 to 2026 totals were checked against the Lawn Tennis Association's prize money page | Total prize fund and singles champions' prizes in pounds, 1968 to 2026. There was no tournament in 2020 |
| `uk_cpi.csv` | Office for National Statistics, series D7BT (CPI all items, 2015 = 100), annual averages | 1988 to 2025. 2026 is not complete, so amounts are adjusted to 2025 prices |
| `wimbledon_2026_singles.csv` | Lawn Tennis Association, "Wimbledon prize money 2026" | Prize per player by the round in which he or she went out. Qualifying has 128 players for 16 places |
| `slams_2026.csv` | Lawn Tennis Association prize money pages (Wimbledon, Roland Garros); Wikipedia, "US Open (tennis)"; Pro Football Network report on the Australian Open, January 2026 | In each event's own currency. The US Open's $101 million is prize money; it reports $108 million including other payments to players |
| `roland_garros_prize_money.csv` | Lawn Tennis Association, "Roland Garros prize money" | Total prize money in euros, 2011 to 2026 |
| `aeltc_accounts.csv` | Sportcal, "Revenue boost for 2024 Wimbledon ensures record surplus passed to LTA", reporting the All England Club's accounts | Years to July. 90% of the surplus goes to the Lawn Tennis Association |

Removed from the earlier version of this page because they could not be traced to a source: estimates of combined Grand Slam revenue since 2000, players' share of revenue, the share of players losing money, broadcasting's share of revenue, a "commercial impact score", a survival simulator, and all confidence ratings and forecasts.
